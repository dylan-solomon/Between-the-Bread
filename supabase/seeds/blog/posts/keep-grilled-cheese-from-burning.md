You flip the sandwich and the bread is nearly black, but when you pull the halves apart the cheese is still stiff and cold. If your grilled cheese comes out burnt on the outside with the cheese not melted, you are not alone. It is one of the most common grilled cheese problems, and it has a simple cause: the bread is cooking much faster than the cheese is melting.

The fix is to slow the bread down and speed the cheese up. This guide covers both, in the order that makes the biggest difference.

## The short version

- Use medium-low heat, not medium-high.
- Cover the pan for a minute or two so trapped heat and steam melt the cheese.
- Use shredded or thinly sliced cheese, taken out of the fridge a few minutes early.
- Do not overfill. A thick pile of cheese needs more time than the bread can give it.
- Spread butter or mayonnaise thinly and evenly, right out to the edges.

## Why the outside burns first

Bread is thin and dry. Wherever it touches a hot pan, its surface browns within a couple of minutes and burns a few minutes after that. The cheese sits in the middle, insulated by two slices of bread, and it has to be warmed all the way through before it softens.

If the pan is hot enough to brown the bread in two minutes, the cheese has only had two minutes to warm up, which is rarely enough. Lowering the heat stretches out the time the bread takes to brown, and that gives the warmth time to reach the middle.

## Turn the heat down

Medium-low is the right place to start for most pans. Preheat for a minute or two, then reduce the heat and let the pan settle before the sandwich goes in. A good sign is butter that melts and foams gently without browning right away.

Plan on roughly three to five minutes per side. If that feels slow, it is working. A grilled cheese that takes its time is far more likely to finish with golden bread and cheese that stretches.

Your pan matters too. Cast iron and other heavy pans store a lot of heat, so they often need a lower setting than a thin nonstick pan. If the bread keeps scorching in a heavy pan, try a lower setting before you change anything else.

## Cover the pan

This is the most useful single trick. After the first flip, put a lid on the pan, or turn a baking sheet over a griddle, for one to two minutes. The trapped warm air and steam heat the top of the sandwich and soften the cheese without adding any more browning to the bread.

Lift the lid to check, and take it off for the last thirty seconds or so to re-crisp the crust.

## Help the cheese melt faster

- **Shred it.** Small pieces have more surface area and melt much sooner than a thick slice. Freshly shredded cheese also tends to melt more smoothly than bagged shreds, which are often coated with a starch or cellulose powder to keep them from sticking together.
- **Take the chill off.** Ten or fifteen minutes on the counter before cooking helps.
- **Spread it almost to the edges.** Cheese that only sits in the center leaves dry corners and an overloaded middle.
- **Choose a good melter.** Some cheeses resist melting however carefully you cook. Our guide to [the best cheeses for grilled cheese](/blog/best-cheese-for-grilled-cheese) explains which ones melt well and which do not.

## Pick the right fat and the right bread

Butter browns beautifully, but its milk solids darken quickly, so it is less forgiving on a hot pan. Mayonnaise, which is mostly oil and egg, spreads very thin and browns evenly, which is why many cooks swear by it. Either works if the heat is low. As the [grilled cheese entry](/sandwiches/grilled-cheese) in our encyclopedia notes, the outside of the bread is usually spread with butter or mayonnaise.

For the bread itself, slices about half an inch thick work well. Very thin slices toast before anything has time to melt, and very thick ones take so long to heat through that the surface darkens. Breads with added sugar or milk brown faster than plain ones, so give them a little extra attention.

## Rescue a sandwich that is already dark

If the bread is browned but the cheese is still stiff, you do not have to start over:

1. Turn the heat to low and cover the pan for a minute or two.
2. Or move the sandwich to a baking sheet in a 325°F (160°C) oven for a few minutes, until the cheese gives when you press gently on the top.
3. Skip the microwave. It will melt the cheese but leave the bread soft and chewy.

## Other melts need the same patience

Any sandwich that has to melt cheese inside or on top follows the same rule: the heat has to reach the cheese before the surface burns. A [croque monsieur](/sandwiches/croque-monsieur) is often finished in the oven so the cheese on top can melt and color gently. A [patty melt](/sandwiches/patty-melt), griddled with cheese over a beef patty and onions, does better with the same lid-and-low-heat approach.

## Quick troubleshooting

- **Bread burns, cheese is cold:** the heat is too high. Lower it and cover the pan.
- **Cheese melts, but the bread is pale:** raise the heat slightly for the last minute, or take the lid off to crisp the crust.
- **Cheese leaks out and burns in the pan:** the sandwich is overfilled. Use a little less cheese and keep it just inside the edges.
- **Making it dairy-free:** vegan cheese behaves differently, so read our [vegan grilled cheese guide](/blog/vegan-grilled-cheese).
