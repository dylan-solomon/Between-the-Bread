A vegan grilled cheese can be wonderful, or it can be a sandwich with a slab of warm, rubbery plastic inside. The difference is usually the cheese, and it is not always obvious from the package. Plant-based cheese is not one product. Brands and formulas vary a great deal, and recipes change over time, so no single brand wins every time.

What you can do is learn to read the label, choose the right type for the job, and cook it in a way that gives dairy-free cheese the best chance of melting. This guide shows you how. Whichever product you pick, try a small amount before you build a whole sandwich around it.

## Why dairy-free cheese melts differently

Dairy cheese melts because its milk proteins and fat soften together as they warm up. Plant-based cheeses have to imitate that with other ingredients, usually plant oils, starches and sometimes nuts or proteins. How well a vegan cheese melts depends almost entirely on its recipe.

## Read the label: what to look for

- **Coconut oil and starch.** Many supermarket slices and shreds are built from plant oil plus a starch such as tapioca, potato or corn. The starch tends to give some stretch and the oil softens as it warms, so these are often the most melty. They can stay firm if underheated, and turn greasy if overheated.
- **Nut-based.** Cashew and almond cheeses are often creamy and full of flavor. They tend to soften and spread rather than stretch, which makes them good for taste but less good for a long cheesy pull. They also contain tree nuts, which matters if anyone is allergic.
- **Other bases.** Soy, pea protein and other plant bases exist too, and their behavior varies widely. If the pack says the cheese melts, treat that as the maker's claim and check it with a small test.
- **Shreds, slices or blocks.** Shreds melt fastest. Blocks are slowest unless you grate or thinly slice them yourself.

## How to get dairy-free cheese to melt

1. **Shred it or slice it thin.** Small pieces warm through faster than a thick slab.
2. **Use medium-low heat and a lid.** Covering the pan traps steam and gentle heat. A few drops of water in the pan beside the sandwich, not on the bread, then a lid, creates steam that helps the cheese soften.
3. **Give it extra time.** Vegan cheeses often need longer than dairy cheese. Patience on low heat is better than turning it up, which can leave the bread burnt and the cheese cold. Our guide to [keeping grilled cheese from burning before the cheese melts](/blog/keep-grilled-cheese-from-burning) covers the heat in detail.
4. **Use two cheeses.** Pair one that melts and stretches with one that brings flavor, just as you would with dairy cheese. Our guide to [the best cheeses for grilled cheese](/blog/best-cheese-for-grilled-cheese) explains the idea.
5. **Add flavor.** Nutritional yeast is a popular way to add a savory, cheesy taste. A thin smear of mustard, a few caramelized onions or a splash of hot sauce also help a mild vegan cheese taste more like the real thing.
6. **Try a sauce.** A thin layer of a dairy-free cheese sauce, or a white sauce made with plant milk, between the slices can make the middle creamier.

## Check the rest of the sandwich

A sandwich is only vegan if everything in it is.

- **Bread.** Many sandwich breads contain milk, butter, honey or egg. Check the ingredients, because they differ from loaf to loaf.
- **Fat.** Use a vegan butter, olive oil or a vegan mayonnaise for the outside, and read the label to be sure.
- **Labels in general.** "Dairy-free" and "vegan" are not the same thing, since vegan also excludes things like honey and egg. Always read labels for allergens too, including nuts, soy and wheat. Ingredients change, and this guide cannot replace the label in your hand.
- **Gluten-free bread as well?** It tends to brown and dry out differently from regular bread, but the same low heat and covered pan help.

## Common problems

- **The cheese is greasy or oily:** the heat is too high. Turn it down.
- **The cheese stays firm or rubbery:** it needs more time covered on low heat, or it needs to be shredded finer.
- **It tastes flat:** add nutritional yeast, mustard or caramelized onions.
- **The bread burns first:** that is a heat problem, not a cheese problem. See the guide linked above.

## Toppings and what to serve

Vegan grilled cheese loves toppings that add moisture and flavor: sliced tomato, spinach, avocado, caramelized onions or roasted peppers. For the side, see [what to serve with grilled cheese](/blog/what-to-serve-with-grilled-cheese), and check the label on any soup, because some contain cream or butter.
