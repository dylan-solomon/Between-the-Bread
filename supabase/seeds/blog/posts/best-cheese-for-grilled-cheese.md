The best cheese for grilled cheese has to do two jobs: it has to melt into a smooth, gooey layer, and it has to taste good when it is warm. Very few cheeses do both perfectly on their own, which is why so many cooks use a blend. Once you know why some cheeses melt and others refuse to, choosing gets easy.

## What makes a cheese melt

Four things matter most: moisture, fat, age and acidity.

- **Moisture and fat.** Younger, moister, fairly high-fat cheeses soften and flow readily.
- **Age.** As cheese ages it loses moisture and its protein network tightens. Instead of flowing, an old cheese tends to split into firm bits and grease.
- **Acidity and how it is made.** Some cheeses, such as halloumi and paneer, are made in a way that keeps them firm when heated. Very fresh, crumbly cheeses such as feta just soften.

## The best melting cheeses

- **Cheddar.** The classic. Mild to medium cheddar melts smoothly. Very sharp, long-aged cheddar has more flavor but can turn oily and grainy, so it is best blended with something milder.
- **American.** A processed cheese made with emulsifying salts, so it melts as smoothly and creamily as any cheese can. The flavor is mild, which makes it a good gooey base.
- **Monterey Jack and Colby.** Mild, buttery and quick to melt. Pepper jack is Monterey Jack with chilies, if you want some heat.
- **Gruyère.** Nutty and full of flavor, and it melts well. It is a favorite for French-style sandwiches.
- **Fontina.** Creamy, slightly earthy and very good at melting.
- **Havarti.** Soft and buttery, and it melts smoothly.
- **Provolone.** Mild to sharp, and it stretches nicely.
- **Low-moisture mozzarella.** Very stretchy, but quite mild, so pair it with something with more flavor. Fresh mozzarella releases water as it heats, so pat it dry or your bread may turn soggy.
- **Swiss and Emmental.** Nutty, slightly sweet and good melters, though they can turn stringy.
- **Muenster and young Gouda.** Mild and easy melters.

As our [grilled cheese entry](/sandwiches/grilled-cheese) notes, cheddar and American are the most common fillings, though provolone and Gouda are also used.

## Combinations that work

A blend lets one cheese bring the flavor and the other bring the melt.

- **Sharp cheddar and American:** the flavor of the first with the creaminess of the second.
- **Cheddar and Gruyère:** sharp and nutty together.
- **Provolone and sharp cheddar:** stretchy with a bit of bite.
- **Fontina with a little grated Parmesan:** the fontina melts, and Parmesan pressed into the buttered outside of the bread browns into a crisp, savory crust.
- **Monterey Jack and pepper jack:** easy melting with a gentle kick.

## Cheeses that don't melt well

These are not bad cheeses. They just do a different job.

- **Aged hard cheeses** such as Parmesan, Pecorino and aged Manchego soften and brown, but they do not turn gooey. Use them as a sprinkle for flavor and crunch, not as the main cheese.
- **Feta, queso fresco and cotija** crumble and soften instead of flowing.
- **Halloumi and paneer** hold their shape when heated. They are wonderful fried or grilled, but they will not give you a melty pull.
- **Fresh goat cheese, ricotta and cream cheese** warm up and turn creamy, but they do not stretch. They are fine as an addition for tang, not as the main cheese.
- **Blue cheese** softens but is intense, so a little goes a long way.
- **Bagged pre-shredded cheese** is often coated with a starch or cellulose powder so the shreds do not clump, which can make it melt a little less smoothly than cheese you shred yourself.

## Getting the most out of any cheese

- **Shred it.** Small pieces melt quickly and evenly.
- **Take the chill off.** Ten or fifteen minutes on the counter helps it melt before the bread browns.
- **Keep the heat low.** Even the best melting cheese will lose to a hot pan, so read our guide on [how to keep grilled cheese from burning before the cheese melts](/blog/keep-grilled-cheese-from-burning).

## Cheese in other classic sandwiches

The same qualities explain the cheeses in famous sandwiches. A classic [croque monsieur](/sandwiches/croque-monsieur) is typically made with Gruyère or a similar Alpine cheese. A [Reuben](/sandwiches/reuben) usually calls for Swiss, which melts well over corned beef and sauerkraut. And [mozzarella in carrozza](/sandwiches/mozzarella-in-carrozza), the Italian fried mozzarella sandwich, is built around exactly the stretchy melt that mozzarella is known for.
